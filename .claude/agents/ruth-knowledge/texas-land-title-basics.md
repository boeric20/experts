# Texas title research basics

Not legal advice. Title opinions come from a Texas attorney, and title commitments and
policies from licensed title insurance agents or underwriters. Ruth supports research and
keeps the evidence.

## Where records are
- Instruments affecting land (deeds, deeds of trust, releases, easements, leases or their
  memoranda, affidavits) are recorded with the county clerk in the real property or official
  public records, indexed by grantor and grantee.
- Probate files are kept by the clerk of the court that handled the estate (usually the
  county clerk). Wills admitted to probate and related orders are often also recorded in the
  real property records.
- Original land grants and survey files: Texas General Land Office.
- Appraisal district (CAD) records show who is taxed, not who holds title. Owner names and
  deed references on the CAD are leads to confirm at the clerk.

## Running a chain
- Start from the current vesting deed and work back through each grantor, noting instrument
  type, parties, date signed, date recorded, recording reference (volume/page or instrument
  number), legal description, and reservations or exceptions.
- Run each owner forward in the grantor index for the time they held title, to catch deeds
  of trust, easements, partial conveyances, mineral conveyances, and double conveyances.
- Track every link's evidence; a gap is recorded as a gap, never filled by assumption.

## Common gaps and how they are usually addressed
- Owner died: probate (including muniment of title in Texas), a court determination of
  heirship, or a recorded affidavit of heirship. Check the current Texas Estates Code text
  before describing any requirement.
- Name variations, missing releases, unrecorded conveyances, boundary conflicts.
- Foreclosure (substitute trustee's deed), tax sale (sheriff's or constable's deed), divorce
  decrees, entity mergers or forfeiture: each needs its own supporting records.
- Severed minerals and reserved interests: the surface owner may not own the minerals.
  Treat surface and minerals as separate chains unless the user limits the search to one.

## How to present ownership findings
- Every ownership statement links to its record and has a status: unverified, record found,
  reviewed by a person, attorney opinion.
- Show the appraisal-district owner and the record owner separately when they differ.
