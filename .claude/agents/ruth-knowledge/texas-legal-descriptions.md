# Texas legal descriptions

Texas does not use the federal township-and-range system. Land is described by reference to
original surveys.

## Survey references
- Original surveys are named for the original grantee or the entity granted the land, and
  carry an abstract number assigned within each county (for example "A-368 SCOTT JAMES SUR
  #118": abstract 368, the James Scott Survey, survey number 118). The same abstract number
  means different land in another county.
- Railroad and school land surveys in West Texas are commonly described by section and block
  (for example "Section 12, Block 3, H&TC RR Co Survey"), sometimes with a township
  ("Block 48, T-9, T&P Ry").
- Subdivisions: lot and block in a recorded plat, with the plat's recording reference.
- The General Land Office holds the original survey files and county abstract maps. The
  Railroad Commission Public GIS Viewer has a survey layer useful for cross-checks.

## Metes and bounds
- A sequence of calls (bearing and distance) from a point of beginning, often tied to a
  survey corner. Check closure (does the last call return to the start) and compare
  calculated area with the called acreage.
- Units: 1 vara = 33 1/3 inches (2.7778 ft); 1 acre = about 5,645.4 square varas;
  1 labor = about 177.1 acres; 1 league = about 4,428.4 acres. Confirm the unit stated in the
  instrument.

## Matching a description to a mapped tract
- Appraisal district shapes are approximations; they do not replace a survey.
- Match on county, abstract and survey, section and block, then acreage and shape. Report the
  match strength and every mismatch.
- Show called (deeded) acreage and any calculated acreage side by side; never reconcile them
  silently.
- Label any plotted calls "plotted from description, not surveyed". Flag descriptions that do
  not close or that conflict with a prior instrument; do not resolve them.
