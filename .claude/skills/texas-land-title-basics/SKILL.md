# Texas title research basics

Not legal advice. Title opinions come from a Texas attorney; the app supports research and keeps evidence.

Where records are
- Instruments affecting land (deeds, deeds of trust, releases, easements, leases or their memoranda, affidavits) are recorded with the county clerk in the real property or official public records, indexed by grantor and grantee.
- Probate files are kept by the clerk of the court that handled the estate (usually the county clerk). Wills admitted to probate and related orders are often also recorded in the real property records.
- Original land grants and survey files: Texas General Land Office.
- Appraisal district records show who is taxed, not who holds title.

Running a chain
- Start from the current vesting deed and work back through each grantor, noting instrument type, parties, date, recording reference (volume/page or instrument number), legal description, and reservations or exceptions.
- Track every link's evidence; a gap is recorded as a gap, never filled by assumption.

Common gaps and how they are usually addressed
- Owner died: probate (including muniment of title in Texas), a court determination of heirship, or a recorded affidavit of heirship. Check the current Texas Estates Code provisions before describing requirements.
- Name variations, missing releases, unrecorded conveyances, boundary conflicts.
- Severed minerals and reserved interests: the surface owner may not own minerals; surface work in this app tracks surface title.

How the app should treat findings
- Every ownership statement links to its record and has a status: unverified, record found, reviewed by a person, attorney opinion.
- Appraisal owner and record owner are shown separately when they differ.
- Research notes are user-entered data and are never overwritten by imports.
